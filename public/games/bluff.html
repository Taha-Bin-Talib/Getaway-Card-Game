Bluff

What is Bluff?

Bluff is a card game about deception. Everyone tries to empty their hand, but you can only get rid of cards by claiming what they are. You place cards face down on a shared pile and announce their rank. You might be telling the truth, or you might be sneaking in cards that do not match. Anyone at the table may challenge you. If you were lying, you pick up the whole pile. If you were honest, the challenger does.

The game appears in many households under names such as Cheat, I Doubt It, or simply BS. The rules vary a little from place to place, and this guide describes the version you can play on Getaway Card Game.

Setup

Bluff works with 3 to 10 players. A standard 52-card deck is used, and for bigger groups a second deck is shuffled in, so that there are enough cards of each rank to make lies believable. All cards are dealt out. The holder of the Ace of Spades starts the first round.

How a round works

  1. The opener places between one and four cards face down on the pile and names a single rank, for example "two Aces". The cards do not have to match the claim.
  2. The next player, clockwise, has three choices: call bluff on the last play, add one or two more face-down cards to the pile for the same rank (making the claim bigger, such as "three Aces"), or pass.
  3. If a player adds cards, the next player faces the same three choices, and so on around the table.
  4. If every other player passes, the pile is thrown away for the rest of the game and a new round begins.

The rank claimed by the opener stays the same throughout the round. Everyone who adds cards is claiming those cards are the same rank. That is what makes bluffing necessary: if you run out of the right rank, you must either pass or lie.

Calling a bluff

When you call a bluff, the cards that the last player added to the pile are turned face up. If every one of those cards matches the claimed rank, the claim was honest and you, the caller, pick up the entire pile. If even one card does not match, the claim was a lie and the player who played it picks up the entire pile. Whoever picks up the pile opens the next round.

Calling is a gamble. A wrong call is expensive, because the pile can contain dozens of cards, but letting a liar get away with it takes you closer to defeat as other players empty their hands.

The extra turn: what happens when everyone passes

Our version includes a rule that keeps the pressure on. When every other player passes on a play, the player who made that play gets one more chance to add cards. They cannot call their own play, but they may add one or two more cards for the same rank, which gives everyone else another chance to respond. If they add again and everyone passes again, they get yet another turn. The pile is thrown away only when the player who made the last play also passes, which means a round of N players has N−1 passes from the others and one more from the player themselves. This makes strong plays much more effective, because a player who holds many cards of the claimed rank can keep shedding them honestly.

Winning and losing

If you empty your hand without being caught, you are safe and leave the game. The game continues until only one player still holds cards, and that player loses. Note that you must survive the call that follows your last play: if you played your final cards and they were called as a bluff, you pick the pile back up.

Strategy tips

Count the cards

There are only four copies of each rank in a single deck (eight with two decks). If you hold three Kings and someone claims three more, you know something is wrong. Counting what you can see is the surest way to find a liar.

Lie small

The best bluffs add a single wrong card to a group of honest ones. Claiming three Queens with two real Queens and one stray card is much harder to challenge than claiming four of a rank you do not hold at all.

Remember who was caught

If a player picked up the pile after a failed call, they now hold a lot of cards, including some they have just shown. Players with big hands are often forced to lie, so their claims deserve more suspicion.

Do not call too often

A confident liar relies on others being afraid of the pile. If you call every claim, you will sooner or later call an honest one and collect the pile. Call when the numbers do not add up, not when you simply feel uneasy.

Use the extra turn

If you opened with an honest group and everyone passes, use your extra turn to add the remaining cards of that rank. You shed cards safely and force the table to decide again.

Other names for the game

Bluff belongs to a family of games that has travelled the world under many names, including Cheat, I Doubt It and BS. They share the same heart: put cards down, say what they are, and risk being called a liar. If you know a house rule that differs from the one on this page, let us know on the blog; we like hearing how families play.

Frequently asked questions

Can I add cards of a different rank?

You may physically place any cards, but you claim they are the rank of the round. If they are not, that is a bluff and you risk being caught.

How many cards can I play at once?

The opener plays one to four cards. Anyone adding to the pile plays one or two.

Can I call a bluff on my own play?

No. If everyone else passes you get an extra turn to add more cards, but you can only add or pass.

Who loses the game?

The last player still holding cards after everyone else has emptied their hands safely.
