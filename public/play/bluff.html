Play Bluff

Put cards face down, say what they are, and call out anyone you think is lying. Play online with 3 to 8 friends.

How to play
  • 3 to 8 players. All cards are dealt; the Ace of Spades holder opens.
  • Play 1–4 cards face down and name a rank, for example “two Aces”. The cards don’t have to match.
  • The next player can call bluff, add 1–4 more cards, or pass.
  • A called play is turned over: a lie means its player picks up the pile, the truth means the caller does.
  • If everyone else passes, the last player to add gets another go before the pile is thrown away.
  • Empty your hand to be safe. The last player holding cards loses.

    About Bluff online

    Bluff, also known as Cheat or I Doubt It, is a card game about nerve and deception. Everyone tries to get rid of their cards, but you can only play them by claiming what they are. Put one to four cards face down, name a rank such as “two Aces”, and hope nobody calls you out. This page lets you play it in real time with three to ten friends, right in your browser.

    Each player in turn can call bluff, add one to four more face-down cards for the same rank, or pass. If the call is right, the liar picks up the whole pile; if it is wrong, the caller does. Our version adds an extra turn: when everyone else passes, the player who made the last play gets another chance to add cards before the pile is thrown away. Read the full rules and strategy guide for the details.

    Features

    • Rooms for 3 to 8 players with a four-letter code and a one-tap invite link.
    • Oval table where every player sits around the pile and the current turn is highlighted.
    • Fair server-side shuffle and full rule enforcement, so nobody can cheat for real.
    • One-minute timer per move so a slow player cannot hold up the game.
    • Two decks automatically for larger groups, so lies stay believable.
    • Room chat and emoji reactions that float over the table.
    • Automatic play for dropped players; reopen the page to rejoin.

    Tips for your first game

    Lie small: one wrong card hidden among honest ones is much harder to challenge than four cards you do not own. Count the cards you can see, and remember that a player who just picked up the pile now holds many cards and is often forced to bluff.
